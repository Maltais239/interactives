    Build with point controls or a keyboard

    Tap two grid points to connect them. Road pieces belong on the dashed road line; wood and steel provide bracing. To turn a flat beam into Road, choose Road and connect the same two neighbouring points. With the grid focused, use arrow keys to choose a point and Enter to connect; Escape cancels. Press 1 for Road, 2 for Wood, 3 for Steel, 4 for Erase, and 5 for Cable. Beams meet only at their endpoints.

    Road first, then bracing.

    Connect all eight Road sections to make the driving surface. Highlighted gaps need Road, even if a wood or steel beam is there. Use Wood or Steel beams, or Cable, to support it. Cables pull; a compressed cable goes slack. The gold anchors are fixed to the banks.

    Start with the car on a road-only deck. Build your own bracing. Heavier vehicles unlock one at a time through the tank. Then try five design missions: an efficient truss, steel beam, arch, suspension and a gusty-wind finale. Each level has design hints below the building tools. Press Test Bridge. Watch bending, stress and cable tension. A red piece is at its limit.

    03

    Change one thing.

    View completed beam, truss, arch and suspension examples for ideas, then build your own supports. Examples cannot be loaded into your bridge. After a win, choose Next challenge to keep your bridge and try the next mission. Your bridge stays with you; design missions may ask you to rebuild it in a new shape. Compare load, peak stress, bending and cost in your notebook. A crossing completes the level only when the required vehicle crosses within its budget and meets any structure or wind goal. Over-budget designs can be tested, but they do not complete the challenge. Free build has no budget limit.

    What does this model show?

    Road, Wood and Steel use a linear 2D beam frame. Cable uses a geometrically nonlinear, tension-only axial model: it has no bending or compression capacity. Cables are installed with a small preset stretch; the model then settles their shape under the load. Strength, cost, wind and vehicle weight use relative classroom units. Stress combines bending and axial load; long compressed pieces have a lower buckling limit. The fall after a break is an animation. This is an investigation model, not a real bridge safety tool.

      Why do these designs work?

      Triangles stay in shape

      A rectangle can lean into a diamond. A diagonal closes it into triangles, giving forces a route through the beams.

      Depth fights bending

      Separate the road and a second row of beams. Connect them with verticals and braces so they work together as a deeper structure.

      Push and pull differ

      Compression pushes a member together; tension pulls it apart. A long compressed beam may buckle. A cable can only carry tension.

      Joints complete the route

      Connect beams at shared endpoints, and lead the load to the gold bank anchors. Pieces that only cross in the middle do not join.

A lesson built around decisions

Students compare individual choice and shared responsibility, identify values in examples, and defend a policy using evidence and a counterargument.

A 35–50 minute route

  1. Learn, 8 minutes: compare the two lenses and explore the values. Ask how a public library can support individual freedom.
  2. Decide, 12–15 minutes: pairs choose policies for two or more fictional scenarios. Ask who benefits, who pays, and what a critic might say.
  3. Sort, 5–8 minutes: classify twelve examples. The final two deliberately combine values.
  4. Write, 10–15 minutes: use a scenario to write a claim, connect a value, cite an example, and respond to a concern. Print or copy the work.

What to assess

Assess accurate use of concepts, a clear example, recognition of a tradeoff and a reasoned response. Policy choices have no correct political answer. The choices summary describes these scenarios; it does not label a student’s ideology.

Curriculum and sources

Designed for the individualism and collectivism content in Alberta’s Grade 8 draft social studies curriculum, available for optional piloting in 2026–27. This is an introductory lesson, not coverage of the whole ideology, economic systems or political systems unit.

The history activity uses short, attributed excerpts from Locke’s Second Treatise, Rousseau’s The Social Contract and Mill’s On Liberty. Each excerpt links to its full primary text. Plain-language explanations are interpretations; the authors are not assigned scores on a modern political spectrum.

Access and classroom use

Buttons support keyboard and touch. Sorting also supports dragging; keys 1, 2 and 3 choose a category while on the sorting view. Read aloud uses the browser’s available voices. Illustrations depict fictional modern settings and were generated for this activity; they are not historical evidence.

Progress is saved locally where browser storage is available. Save project creates a portable JSON file. No account, API key or server submission is needed. Make it my own downloads the HTML with the illustrations, lesson data, styles and app code embedded.
